## 01 / EVALUATING ARCHITECTURAL OPTIONS

### Choose with *context.*

For a new backend integration, I led the technical design from the initial requirements through to a proposed architecture.

I compared different implementation approaches, considering infrastructure costs alongside integration constraints, operational needs and long-term maintainability. I defined component responsibilities and the interfaces needed to fit the solution into an existing environment, then presented the approach for technical review.

I enjoy this part of engineering because the most appropriate solution is not necessarily the most elaborate one. The decision needs to make sense for the system, the team and the problem being solved.

## 02 / TURNING DESIGN INTO SOFTWARE

### Make decisions *buildable.*

After the design was agreed upon, I broke the work into development tasks and helped turn the architecture into a working implementation.

I contributed code alongside other engineers, including through pair programming, and worked closely with Product to keep the technical approach aligned with the intended behavior.

The solution was built but ultimately did not reach production because the wider project changed direction. It remains a meaningful example of taking ownership of technical design while staying directly involved in development.

## 03 / DESIGNING FOR CHANGE

### Think beyond the *next release.*

I have also worked on introducing new capabilities alongside established backend systems that were expected to evolve.

Rather than expanding the responsibilities of an existing application, I advocated for a clearer boundary between the established system and the new functionality. The goal was to avoid creating additional work for a future transition while allowing the new component to evolve on its own.

I helped make that direction concrete through early technical foundations, before other engineers continued the feature development. It was a pragmatic trade-off between short-term delivery effort and longer-term change.

## MY APPROACH

### Thoughtful choices, *hands-on delivery.*

I like making trade-offs explicit, testing ideas against real constraints and keeping architecture connected to implementation.

For me, good design is not about adding more structure. It is about making software easier to change, understand and maintain.
